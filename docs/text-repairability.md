# Text Repairability

The deck is a visual draft. Design all text so later manual correction is easy even without rebuilding the entire slide.

## Core requirement

Every text-bearing region must sit on a visually uniform, single solid-color local background.

This includes:

- page titles and subtitles;
- colored section/header bands;
- card headers;
- body copy;
- diagram labels;
- callouts;
- numeric indicators;
- captions;
- small tags.

A nearly-flat or subtle gradient still counts as a gradient and **fails** when it sits behind text.

Do not place text over:

- gradients;
- textures;
- patterns;
- photographs;
- illustrations;
- translucent overlays;
- decorative linework;
- arrows/connectors;
- chart marks;
- complex diagram detail.

## Margin requirement

Keep generous negative space around every text block.

Text must not:

- touch the edge of its local background zone;
- collide with icons or diagrams;
- intersect arrows or connectors;
- sit inside visually congested regions.

## Overlay test

Imagine covering a text block with a solid rectangle roughly 20% larger than the visible text bounds in every direction.

The slide passes only if that rectangle can cover the original text without damaging:

- nearby graphics;
- important borders;
- arrows/connectors;
- icons;
- charts;
- neighboring text.

The **entire expanded rectangle** should sit on a uniform local fill. It is not enough for only the glyph area itself to look clean.

## Header-band test

Text-bearing header bars are a common failure mode.

If a header contains text, its fill must be flat and locally uniform. Do not use a blue-to-cyan, light-to-dark, glossy, photographic, or textured header behind text.

Shadows may exist outside the repair zone, but should not make the text area itself non-uniform.

## Sample preflight

Because the approved sample propagates visual habits to the entire deck, check every sample title/header/label for repairability **before** showing it for approval.

Do not let a gradient text band or connector-through-label become part of the deck's primary style anchor.

## Important clarification

Repair-safe text does **not** mean every text block must be placed inside a visible card.

Preferred solutions include:

- clean white open space;
- large flat-color areas;
- restrained flat-color callout zones;
- uncluttered diagram-label areas.

Gradients, illustrations, photography, and rich decorative treatments remain allowed elsewhere on the slide, outside the repair-safe text zones.
